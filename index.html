Layer 02 · Research

Research

Research paper · Oct 2026 · Spies Lab, CSUCI

Benthic macroinvertebrate community change following the 2025 Palisades Fire in Topanga Lagoon

Compared benthic macroinvertebrate assemblages before and after the January 2025 Palisades Fire in Topanga Lagoon, critical habitat for the federally endangered northern tidewater goby. Taxon richness fell from 20 taxa before the fire to as few as 2, and Shannon diversity dropped from 1.74 to 0.34, showing severe simplification of the community and incomplete recovery more than a year later. The paper also outlines a GIS-based follow-up study on goby occurrences beyond the mapped critical habitat unit.

Chris Blackwell, Devan Page, Tatyana Ramirez, Jet Thompson, and Brenton Spies

SURF research poster · Spies Lab, CSUCI

Post-fire recovery of Topanga Canyon Lagoon: monitoring benthic macroinvertebrates and endangered tidewater goby populations

Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship poster with the Resource Conservation District of the Santa Monica Mountains. Ostracods were the first taxa to recolonize after the fire and likely sustained goby prey availability, while pollution-sensitive Hydropsychidae have not returned. The macroinvertebrate trend closely mirrors the goby population, with a sharp post-fire drop and a recovery spike beginning in fall 2025.

Layer 03 · Capstone

Capstone: mapping occupied tidewater goby habitat

In progress · Follow-up to the Topanga Lagoon research

During field surveys, our team observed northern tidewater gobies upstream of the boundary of the designated critical habitat unit. If confirmed, the current designation may not capture the full extent of habitat the species uses. My capstone is a GIS study testing whether occupied reaches extend beyond current protection zones, and identifying the physical features that limit upstream access.

  1. Compile occurrence data. Assemble permitted goby survey records with precise coordinates, dates, methods, effort, and life stages, and compare them against the official USFWS critical habitat geometry after checking coordinate systems and positional accuracy.
  2. Model stream gradient. Use LiDAR-derived elevation data to build a conditioned stream network and calculate reach-scale channel slope, since the federal designation identifies steep gradient as one possible upstream limit.
  3. Field-verify limiting features. Survey candidate upper limits for passage barriers such as culverts and drops, dry reaches, flow permanence, substrate, and depth, repeated across seasons.
  4. Map and report. Produce separate layers for observed occupancy, potentially suitable habitat, and designated critical habitat, with sensitivity analyses for elevation and slope.

The maps and technical report will be submitted to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service for consideration and prepared for peer-reviewed publication. Only USFWS can revise a critical habitat designation; this study is meant to provide the spatial evidence for that review.
